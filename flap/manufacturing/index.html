1. The stack

  • Established Faster spin on the first and top layers makes them thinner, which strengthens the attraction between electrodes after rolling. This is the ½D.
  • Proposed, untested Two active layers on each panel: design study. One active layer if weight is critical.

3. Electrode filters

Established

  1. Disperse 150 µL CNT solution (Invisicon 3500) in 20 mL isopropyl alcohol. Sonicate for 2 min.
  2. Pour over a 90 mm PTFE filter (Sartorius 7022P) and pull the solvent through by vacuum. Areal density: 2.36 µL/cm².
  3. Dry at room temperature for 48 h.

To confirm There is a trade-off: more CNT lowers resistance but lowers breakdown field. Use the lowest concentration that gives the bandwidth you need, which for 2 Hz flapping is low. Larger area also means slower charging.

5. Rolling the trailing edge

Established A planar sheet is cut and rolled, and caps with copper wires make the contacts.

Proposed, untested Here, only the 13 mm edge strip is rolled, stopping at the fibre line, so the fibre stays part of the sheet with no glue line. Roll straight: a fibre that wanders stretches unevenly.

To confirm Contacts at the two tabs: caps and copper wire, or silver ink.

6. Stretching

  • Proposed, untested Stretch each panel before bonding. Hold it away from its outline, in the model at least 20 mm from the grips, which gives a variation in tension of 2.6 %.
  • Proposed, untested 8 % along the fibre: panel 1's fibre goes from 52.0 to 56.2 mm. Hold the width at 0.2 %, which is close to its unstretched width, so the sheet cannot neck.
  • Proposed, untested Do not pull only on the fibre tips: the model leaves 70–86 % of the sheet slack. Do not move the LE after bonding: panel 2 wrinkles.
  • Proposed, untested Print markers before stretching, to measure the stretch you actually got. Lift change is fairly tolerant of ±1 % in the model, but measure it.
  • To confirm CNT and stretch: pre-stretching CNT electrodes lowers conductivity, and the established process uses no pre-stretch. Measure resistance before and after 8 %.

7. Assembly

To scale: batten tip at $x$ = 70 mm, chord 41 mm, 104 mm span.

  1. Proposed, untested Bond the stretched sheet to the LE (2 mm margin) and batten: a tube of 3 mm outer and 2 mm inner diameter for the LE, about 0.5–0.8 mm carbon for the batten. A 1 mm solid LE reached no steady state in the model.
  2. Proposed, untested Cut the sheet free. The fibre should bow in by about 1 mm.
  3. Proposed, untested Bond panel 2 to the same batten, at about the same tension as panel 1.
  4. Proposed, untested LE to the crank. Trailing edge to the body at the fibre's root end: a line with a soft spring, or a DEA fibre rolled from a spare rectangle. In the model a 60 mN DEA fibre matches a rigid root rib (12.3 mN against 12.2 mN), a line gives 8.6–9.6 mN.
  5. Proposed, untested Wiring: tie panel 1's contact B and panel 2's contact A at the junction, down the trailing edge. Panel 2's other contact runs inside the LE, panel 1's at the root. The carbon rod is conductive: keep high voltage away from it, or use it as the grounded side.

10. Main risks

  • Scale. The established process makes small pieces rolled into stiff shells. A 50 × 60 mm stretched free sheet of 105 µm is new, and uniformity and release are unknown.
  • Stretching CNT electrodes. Conductivity falls with stretch.
  • Defects over a large area. Self-clearing helps, but one short could disable a panel. This is why each panel has its own drive.
  • Creep. Tension may relax after stretching. Repeat the resonance test after a day and after a week.
  • Active fibre. The least checked part of the model. Build one panel and test it before a full wing.
