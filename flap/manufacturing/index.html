Making the full membrane wing

A multilayer sheet, spin-coated with carbon nanotube electrodes, cut tilted and unstretched, then tensioned by bringing its root edge and batten to vertical.

3. Electrode filters

Established

  1. Disperse 150 µL CNT solution (Invisicon 3500) in 20 mL isopropyl alcohol. Sonicate for 2 min.
  2. Pour over a 90 mm PTFE filter (Sartorius 7022P) and pull the solvent through by vacuum. Areal density: 2.36 µL/cm².
  3. Dry at room temperature for 48 h.

To confirm There is a trade-off: more CNT lowers resistance but lowers breakdown field. Use the lowest concentration that gives the bandwidth you need, which for 2 Hz flapping is low. Larger area also means slower charging.

5. Rolling the trailing edge

Established A planar sheet is cut and rolled, and caps with copper wires make the contacts.

Proposed, untested Here, only the 20 mm edge strip is rolled, flat and unstretched, from its free edge towards the sheet, stopping at the fibre line, so the fibre stays part of the sheet with no glue line. The 15 mm passive core goes first, and the 5 mm active band ends up as the outer turn; the roll is about 1.6 mm across. Roll straight: a fibre that wanders stretches unevenly.

To confirm Contacts at the two tabs: caps and copper wire, or silver ink.

6. Tilted cut, then rotate to vertical

  1. Proposed, untested Bond the LE as cut. Lay the rolled, unstretched sheet on a template with the outline printed. Bond the LE rod (2 mm margin). The sheet has seen no stretch so far, so the roll and the LE joint are made at rest.
  2. Proposed, untested Add the rods at their as-cut tilts. Root rod at 7.5°, batten at 4.5°, each hinged at the LE, with panel 1 and panel 2 both bonded to the batten. About 0.5–0.8 mm carbon.
  3. Proposed, untested Set the chord stretch. The mask's chord is 39.1 mm and the rods are 41.1 mm, so draw each fibre about 2 mm aft and fix it to the rod tips.
  4. Proposed, untested Pull the root rod to vertical with the line, against a printed square. The batten should arrive at vertical by itself, because it is balanced at this tilt ratio.
  5. Proposed, untested Check before going further: line force about 81 mN (108 mN with three active layers), fibres 4.7 and 4.5 % longer by markers, batten within about 1° of vertical.

7. Assembly

To scale, after rotation: batten at $x$ = 60 mm, chord 41 mm, 104 mm span.

  1. Proposed, untested LE: a tube of 3 mm outer and 2 mm inner diameter. A 1 mm solid LE reached no steady state in the model, on the earlier design.
  2. Proposed, untested LE to the crank. Root rod's tip to the body by an adjustable line, pulling inboard along the span. The tip is 3.8 mm from the stroke axis, so an eye on the axis at the same chord station keeps the line's length constant over a stroke. Run the line through the eye to an adjuster, with a soft spring if needed. Not modelled over a stroke.
  3. Proposed, untested Loads to design for, two layers: 81 mN in the line, and fibre forces of 24 and 23 mN at the rod tips, with almost no moment on the batten when it is vertical.
  4. Proposed, untested If the batten drifts in flow, lock it once vertical. Its balance was computed with no flow. In flow it was held vertical in the model.
  5. Proposed, untested Wiring: tie panel 1's contact B and panel 2's contact A at the junction, down the trailing edge. Panel 2's other contact runs inside the LE, panel 1's at the root. The carbon rods are conductive: keep high voltage away from them, or use them as the grounded side.

Repeatability

Proposed, untested Tension here is set by geometry, so a repeatable wing comes from locating parts, not from skill or from reading a force. Use hard stops for every position that matters, one datum from the mask to the fixture, and forces only as checks. Aim: lift change and fibre force each within ±10 % between wings.

Trailing edge

  • Roll against a fence on the fibre line. Half a millimetre of roll is 1.2 % chord stretch, so where the roll stops also sets the chord.
  • Start straight: lay a fine thread on the free edge as an axis. Check diameter at five stations (±5 %) and straightness (0.2 mm over the fibre).
  • Set fibre length with a notch or slotted cap at each rod tip, and keep glue from wicking along the fibre.
  • Mark two points on each fibre and measure their spacing after rotation: 4.7 % and 4.5 %, ±0.4 %.
  • Pluck test between wings: frequency goes with $\sqrt{F}$, so ±5 % in frequency is ±10 % in force. Use it as a fingerprint, not an absolute.

Leading edge and rods

  • Fiducials in the electrode layer at the LE, fibre and rod lines, on the passive margin, so each wing carries its own target.
  • Keep the film on its carrier until the LE is bonded. Tube in a straight groove, fiducial aligned to the tube under a microscope, ±0.1 mm.
  • Control the adhesive: stencilled bead on the margin, fixed cure time and weight. Compare adhesives and primer on peel coupons first.
  • Hinge block with two hard stops, at the cut tilt and at vertical. A stop 30 mm from the hinge with 0.1 mm error is 0.19°.
  • Lock the batten once vertical. Its balance was computed with no flow.
  • Keep the rod seated on its stop, and read the line force only as a check: about 81 mN, ±10 %.
  • Measure at a fixed age. Same wait after rotation and same number of voltage cycles, then repeat after a day and a week.

Order of work

  1. Build the fixture and add the fiducials.
  2. Make coupons: peel on tube and rod, and a rolled strip.
  3. Build three one-panel wings the same way. Their spread is the real tolerance.
  4. Find the largest source of spread before changing anything.
  5. Only then change the edge or add the second panel.

10. Main risks

  • Scale. The established process makes small pieces rolled into stiff shells. A 50 × 60 mm free sheet of 105 µm is new, and uniformity and release are unknown.
  • Stretching CNT electrodes. Conductivity falls with stretch, and panel 1 also shears.
  • Wrinkles at 0 V. Panel 1 is predicted mostly wrinkled at rest. The model cannot say how large they are, or what they do to the flow. Test this first.
  • Hinges and sequence. The bonding order, hinge friction and the batten's balance in flow are not simulated.
  • Defects over a large area. Self-clearing helps, but one short could disable a panel. This is why each panel has its own drive.
  • Creep. Tension may relax after rotation, and the adjustable line can take it up. Repeat the resonance test after a day and after a week.
  • $C_L$ near 1.2 at voltage. Above what attached flow supports at this Reynolds number, so lift change at voltage may be lower than predicted.
