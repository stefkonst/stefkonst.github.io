Active membrane wings / B-FAAV

B-FAAV: 100 cm scale

The FAAV is a 1 m, 250 g flapping robot. At this size a DEA skin cannot change the wing's tension enough, and discrete DEAs in a free-edged skin have no load path. What remains is to keep the wing as it is and set its tension where it attaches to the body. Nothing is built: this page holds a proof-of-concept layout and the numbers behind it.

Proof of concept: tension set at the attachment

Proposal
  • The DEAs sit in the body, in series with the wing's trailing edge, so most of their force goes through the membrane (series rule, Eq. 3).
  • A cord along the edge spreads the pull: a single tie point would only tension the membrane within 50–70 mm of it (Eq. 4).
  • The wing is unchanged, so this works with the original FAAV wings and does not depend on film fabrication.
  • The same switch could set a taut wing in air and a slack one in water, where the dynamic pressure is of the same order (Eq. 6).

Numbers

Calculation

Cruise of the 250 g robot with 0.2 m² of wing at $C_L$ 0.6: $q$ 20 Pa, 5.8 m/s. The wing then needs about 12 N/m of tension ($C_T$ 3) (Eq. 1).

  • One active skin layer changes 0.3–0.8 N/m: 3–7 %. A 20 % change needs 3–8 layers over the whole wing (Eq. 2).
  • At the attachment, a 20 % change needs 8–36 rolled cores per wing, 4–20 g, i.e. 3–16 % of the robot's mass for both wings (Eq. 3, Eq. 5).

Upper end uses 43 mN per core (measured bundle), lower end 200 mN (B-cube-class core, assumed).

Equations

1. Tension the wing needs

$$T_0 = C_T\,q\,c,\qquad q = \frac{m g}{C_L S}$$
  • The wing sits near membrane divergence when $C_T$ is about 2–4. Tension needed grows with chord and with dynamic pressure, so with size.

2. What one active skin layer can change

$$\Delta T_{skin} = \eta\,\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\left(\frac{V}{H}\right)^2 H$$
  • Fixed per unit width by the film alone: about 2.1 N/m ideal at 30 µm and 1.6 kV, of which $\eta$ 0.15–0.4 becomes a drop in chord tension.
  • It does not grow with the wing, so the skin's authority $\Delta T_{skin}/T_0$ falls as $1/c$ at a given speed.

3. Actuators at the attachment

$$\Delta T = \frac{n\,F_{core}}{L_a}\,\frac{k_p}{k_a+k_p},\qquad n = \frac{0.2\,T_0\,L_a}{0.8\,F_{core}}$$
  • $n$ cores in series with the membrane along an edge of length $L_a$. Their force can be sized with the wing, independently of the film, because it scales with the number of cores.

4. A single tie point tensions only nearby membrane

$$T(x) pprox T_{tie}\,e^{-x/\ell},\qquad \ell pprox 50 ext{–}70\ ext{mm}$$
  • Tension spreads from a point load through shear and fades over a length $\ell$ (an exponential approximation of the finite-element result on the 10 cm wing). A cord along the whole edge removes the decay.

5. Mass of the attachment actuators

$$ rac{m_{act}}{m_{robot}} = rac{2\,n\,m_{core}}{m_{robot}}$$
  • With $m_{core}$ = 565 mg (B-cube core, measured) and 8–36 cores per wing: 3–16 % of 250 g.

6. Air against water

$$ rac{q_w}{q_a} = rac{ ho_w U_w^2}{ ho_a U_a^2}$$
  • Swimming at 0.3 m/s against flying at 5.8 m/s gives $q_w/q_a pprox 2.2$: the same order, so one tensioner can serve both media, with $C_T$ (Eq. 1) set by the state of the switch.
