Active membrane wing

A hyperelastic membrane wing with a carbon-rod leading edge, chordwise carbon digits, embedded rolled dielectric elastomer actuators (DEAs) and optional reinforcement strips, loaded by quasi-steady thin-airfoil aerodynamics. Every number on this page is computed live by the same Python file (model.py) that is checked against analytical solutions and compared with the available measurements below. Nothing is animated, fitted on the fly or randomised. It is a model, not measured flight data.

Experiment predictions

Each sweep re-solves the current design point by point (warm-started), so curves can be compared directly with measurements: camber from a side camera or laser line at mid-span, lift, vertical force and thrust from the load cell, DEA strain from markers on one DEA, rod-tip displacement from a top camera, chord tension from the clamp force on the bench (set $U$ = 0). The carbon rod makes the solver slower: sweeps can take minutes in the browser; a 12 × 6 mesh is about three times faster (lift within 1 %, lift change within 4 %, see V7).

Governing equations

Notation follows Konstantinidi et al., Smart Mater. Struct. 32 (2023) 125011 and Compos. Sci. Technol. 258 (2024) 110849. In-plane axes: $x$ spanwise (root to tip), $y$ chordwise (LE to TE); $w$ is the out-of-plane deflection and $\lambda_z$ the thickness stretch. SI units. Each block names the function in model.py that implements it.

1. Kinematics Wing.membrane

Constant-strain triangles, total Lagrangian. The membrane is cut smaller and stretched onto the frame: reference coordinates $X = x_\text{frame}/\lambda_{pre,x}$, $Y = y_\text{frame}/\lambda_{pre,y}$. With the in-plane deformation gradient $\mathbf F$ (3 × 2) and incompressibility $\lambda_x\lambda_y\lambda_z = 1$:

$$\mathbf C=\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}\mathbf F,\qquad \lambda_z^2=\frac{1}{\det\mathbf C},\qquad I_1=\operatorname{tr}\mathbf C+\frac{1}{\det\mathbf C},\qquad I_4=\mathbf a_0\!\cdot\!\mathbf C\,\mathbf a_0=\lambda_F^2$$

with $\mathbf a_0 = [1\;0]$ for the spanwise and $[0\;1]$ for the chordwise family.

2. Strain energy MATERIALS, calibrate.py, fit_p7670.py

$$W_s=v_m\,W_{iso}(I_1)+\sum_{x,y} v_f\,W_{aniso}(I_4),\qquad W_{iso}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}C_i\,(I_1-3)^i,\qquad W_{aniso}=\frac{E_f\,(\sqrt{I_4}-1)^2}{2}\quad(\sqrt{I_4}>1)$$
$$\mathbf S=2\frac{\partial W_s}{\partial I_1}\Big(\mathbf I-\frac{\mathbf C^{-1}}{\det\mathbf C}\Big)+2\frac{\partial W_s}{\partial I_4}\,\mathbf a_0\otimes\mathbf a_0,\qquad s=2(\lambda-\lambda^{-2})\frac{\partial W_s}{\partial I_1}+v_fE_f(\lambda-1)\ \ \text{(uniaxial along }\mathbf a_0)$$

The constants are fitted to measured uniaxial nominal stress (non-negative least squares). The wing membrane is not reinforced with stiff fibres: its anisotropy comes from the compliant silicone features of the composite, and $v_fE_f$ is their effective contribution. Valid to $\lambda = 1.22$. Compressive principal stresses are set to zero (wrinkling; a small residual can be kept for regularisation, default 0).

3. Rolled DEA Wing.bar_forces

Each rolled DEA is a bar of reference section $A_0 = w\,(H+H_p)$ (strip width $w$, dielectric thickness $H$, passive thickness $H_p$, $\chi = H/(H+H_p)$), mounted at pre-stretch $\lambda_{pre,DEA}$. From the equation of state of the ideal DEA, $\sigma_x-\sigma_z=\lambda_x\,\partial W/\partial\lambda_x-\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\chi\lambda_x^2\lambda_y^2 V^2/H^2$, with the roll free to contract laterally ($\lambda_y=\lambda_z=\lambda^{-1/2}$) and the force taken on the current section $A_0/\lambda$:

$$F=A_0\Big[\frac{\partial W_s}{\partial\lambda}-\eta\,\chi\,\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\frac{V^2}{H^2}\Big]=A_0\,2(\lambda-\lambda^{-2})\frac{\partial W_{iso}}{\partial I_1}-\Delta F,\qquad \Delta F=\eta\,\chi\,\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\frac{V^2}{H^2}A_0,\qquad F\ge0$$

$\Delta F$ does not depend on stretch. $\eta$ is the ratio of measured to ideal force change: 0.39–0.41 from the isometric pulses at $\lambda$ = 1.12 (default 0.40), but only 0.03–0.15 from the quasi-static cycles on the same bundle (see C1–C2). At 1.6 kV and $\eta$ = 0.40, $\Delta F$ = 8.46 mN per DEA, against a passive force of 14.1 mN at $\lambda_{pre,DEA}$ = 1.10, so the DEA loses 60 % of its tension. If $F$ reaches zero the DEA is slack and more voltage does nothing (flagged). Breakdown: $E = V\sqrt{\lambda}/H \lt E_{BD} = 100$ V/µm.

4. Reinforcement strips and trailing-edge cord Wing.bar_forces

$$N_f=E_fA_f\,(\lambda-1),\qquad A_f=w_fh_f,\qquad N_f\ge0\ \text{if tension-only (thin strips buckle)}$$

5. Carbon rod and digits (Euler–Bernoulli) Wing.rod_bending

$$EI(x)=E\,\frac{\pi d(x)^4}{64},\qquad d(x)=d_\text{root}+(d_\text{tip}-d_\text{root})\frac{x}{b},\qquad U_b=\sum_i\frac{EI_i\,\ell_i}{2}\,|\boldsymbol\kappa_i|^2,\quad \boldsymbol\kappa_i=a_i\mathbf x_{i-1}+b_i\mathbf x_i+c_i\mathbf x_{i+1}$$

The LE rod is cantilevered at the root; each digit leaves the rod at a stiff joint. The clamp uses a mirrored ghost node $\mathbf x_g=\mathbf x_1-2h\,\mathbf t$ (second-order accurate, V8). Bending is resolved in and out of plane. The rods' axial stiffness is capped for the explicit solver (they are effectively inextensible; V8 shows the result changes by ±4 % for a cap of 50–400 N).

6. Equilibrium Wing.residual

$$\mathbf R_a=\mathbf f^{ext}_a-\sum_e H_m A^e_0\,\mathbf F\mathbf S\,\nabla_{\!X}N_a-\sum_\text{bars}F\,\mathbf e-\frac{\partial U_b}{\partial\mathbf x_a}=\mathbf 0\qquad\text{at every free node}$$

Membrane tension in the current configuration: $\mathbf n = \frac{H_m}{\sqrt{\det\mathbf C}}\,\mathbf F\mathbf S\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}$ (N/m, $H_m$ the membrane thickness); "total chord tension" adds $\sum F_\text{DEA}/b$.

7. Aerodynamics Wing.glauert, Wing.pressure_forces

At each spanwise station the deformed section is referred to its own LE–TE chord ($c_\ell$, $\xi = (y-y_{LE})/(y_{TE}-y_{LE}) = \tfrac12(1-\cos\theta)$, camber line $z_r(\xi)$, local incidence $\alpha_\ell=\alpha+\arctan\frac{w_{LE}-w_{TE}}{y_{TE}-y_{LE}}$). Thin-airfoil theory with the exact integrals of the piecewise-linear camber line:

$$A_0=\alpha_\ell-\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{dz_r}{dy}d\theta,\qquad A_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{dz_r}{dy}\cos n\theta\,d\theta,\qquad \Delta C_p=4\Big[A_0\frac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}+\sum_{n\ge1}A_n\sin n\theta\Big]$$
$$\Delta p=q\,k_{3D}\,e(\eta)\,\Delta C_p,\quad k_{3D}=\frac{AR}{AR+2},\ AR=\frac{2b^2}{S},\quad e(\eta)=\frac4\pi\sqrt{1-\eta^2},\ \eta=\frac{x}{b},\qquad C_L(x)=\pi(2A_0+A_1)\,k_{3D}\,e(\eta)$$

$\Delta C_p$ is averaged exactly over each cell (the LE singularity is integrated, not sampled) and applied as a follower pressure normal to the deformed element. Load and shape are solved together (static aeroelasticity). Attached flow only: warning above 12°.

8. Flapping, quasi-steady Wing.flow_state, dea_active

The pitch angle $\alpha$ (geometric, set by the mounting) and the stroke are independent inputs; the stroke changes the effective angle through the plunge velocity $\Omega x$:

$$\Phi=\Phi_0\cos\varphi,\ \ \Omega=-\Phi_0\omega\sin\varphi,\ \ \dot\Omega=-\Phi_0\omega^2\cos\varphi,\ \ \alpha_\text{eff}(x)=\alpha-\arctan\frac{\Omega x}{U},\ \ q(x)=\tfrac12\rho\big(U^2+\Omega^2x^2\big)$$
$$F_z=\cos\Phi\!\int_0^b q\,c_\ell C_L\cos\gamma\,dx,\qquad F_x=-\!\int_0^b q\,c_\ell C_L\sin\gamma\,dx,\qquad \gamma=\arctan\frac{\Omega x}{U}$$

Membrane, DEA and rod inertia: $f_z=-m\dot\Omega x$, centrifugal $f_x = m\Omega^2x$. Downstroke: $\Omega \lt 0$ ($0 \lt \varphi \lt 180°$). DEA schedules: always on, downstroke only, upstroke only, assuming the DEA follows within a half-stroke. Reduced frequency $k=\pi fc/U$ (warning above 0.2).

9. Solver Wing.solve

Dynamic relaxation with kinetic damping: $m_i\dot{\mathbf v}_i=\mathbf R_i$ with fictitious masses $m_i = 0.6\,S_i$ ($S_i$ a Gershgorin bound on the nodal stiffness), velocities reset at kinetic-energy peaks, load ramped over 400 steps, aerodynamics updated every 10 steps; converged when $\max|\mathbf R| \lt 10^{-4}F_\text{ref}$. The DEA-on state starts from the DEA-off state.

10. Hand check of the authority

Smeared theory replaces the DEAs by a uniform chord tension $T_y+nF/b$. With discrete DEAs at spacing $s=b/n$, the membrane between two DEAs sags under its own spanwise tension $T_x$, and the DEAs control only the sag of the tendon line (tributary width $s$):

$$w_\text{tendon}\approx\frac{p\,s\,c^2}{8\,(F+T_ys)},\qquad w_\text{between}\approx\frac{p\,s^2}{8\,T_x},\qquad \frac{\delta w}{w}\approx\frac{w_\text{tendon}}{w_\text{tendon}+w_\text{between}}\Big[\frac{F+T_ys}{F-\Delta F+T_ys}-1\Big]$$

Case V5 (rigid frame, 0.3 Pa, $F$ = 14.1 mN, $\Delta F$ = 8.5 mN, $T_y$ = 0.96 N/m, $T_x$ = 0.56 N/m, $s$ = 17.5 mm, $c$ = 35 mm): $w_\text{tendon}\approx 26$ µm, $w_\text{between}\approx 20$ µm, so $\delta w/w\approx0.56\times38\,\%\approx21\,\%$. FE: 24.8 %; exact discrete-tendon series: 25.3 %; smeared theory: 32.7 %. Design consequence: closer DEAs (smaller $s$) or a stiffer spanwise membrane put more of the deflection under DEA control.

Known limitations

  • Quasi-steady, attached-flow, strip-wise thin-airfoil aerodynamics with a simple 3D correction: no stall, no wake or added mass, no unsteady circulation.
  • A fully free trailing edge (no cord) has no well-defined static equilibrium in this model: the edge carries no tension and the solution does not converge (a real membrane would luff). The default keeps a thin cord; even a soft one (0.5 GPa) is enough.
  • Membrane bending stiffness neglected; wrinkling by stress clamping, not a full tension-field theory.
  • Calibration: one specimen per direction (n = 1), uniaxial kinematics, up to $\lambda$ = 1.22; P7670 from one bundle of five DEAs; $\eta$ differs by a factor of about three between the two bench methods.
  • The bat armwing (rod LE, digits, free edges with a cord) converges for the default 7 cm wing at 3 m/s, but often not for larger wings at 4–6 m/s (13–20 cm span): the free membrane between flexible digits then has no stable static solution in this explicit solver. A warning appears; those results should not be used. An implicit (Newton) solver or a dynamic model would be needed to tell flutter from a numerical limit.
  • Rectangular or tapered planform only; fibres sit on mesh lines in the membrane mid-plane; the rod–digit joints are stiff. Extra passive silicone from bonding or encapsulation is not added automatically; increase $H_p$ to represent it.
