Which active wing changes lift more?

Two ways of putting dielectric elastomer actuators in a bat-inspired wing, on the same wing size, stroke and voltage, from one analytical model.

These are model predictions. The full membrane has not been built, and $\eta$ of a flat sheet is estimated (0.5), not measured.

Baseline lift about 16 mN per wing at 4 m/s. *The model over-predicts at 6 m/s.

  • Your tested wings: weak results are expected. With a free trailing edge, statics leave no way for voltage to change chord tension.
  • Full against discrete at $\lambda_{pre}$ = 1.03: about 3.3× at 4 m/s, narrowing with speed.
  • But a slack discrete wing may do as well. Lowering $\lambda_{pre}$ raises its lift change to 7.5 mN at 1.01 and 12.7 mN at 1.00, close to or above the full membrane. Those are outside what the model has been checked on, and a slack membrane may flutter. See pre-stretch.

Over the stroke

  • Equation: step 6 at each phase, with $\alpha_{eff}=\alpha-\arctan(\Omega r/U)$ and $q_{eff}=\tfrac12\rho(U^2+\Omega^2r^2)$ at each panel's radius.
  • Trend: a discrete wing changes lift most in the downstroke (2.6 mN) and least in the upstroke (0.5 mN), because its camber follows the load. The full membrane is nearly constant at 6 mN, because voltage removes most of its chord tension whatever the load.
  • Hatched, 240° to 300°, not reliable: the outer panel's angle crosses zero, and it can flip to either side. Left out of the means. Raising the pitch to 8° avoids it: why, and how.
  • Validate: high-speed video and force over a cycle, 0 V and 1.6 kV, same wing. Look for a flat lift change against phase on the full membrane, and for snap-through near 270°.

With speed

  • Equation: step 6, where softening $D$ grows with $q$.
  • Trend: the discrete wing needs high $q$ to act, because its tension stays high: $T$ falls by only 25 % at voltage. The full membrane loses all its chord tension, so it responds at low speed. The ratio falls from about 4.6× at 3 m/s to 3.3× at 4 m/s.
  • Validate: static section in the tunnel, 3, 4, 5, 6 m/s, randomised, with a sham. Above 5 m/s (shaded) the model over-predicts, by about 1.7× on a rigid-edge discrete wing at 6 m/s.

With actuator efficiency

  • Equation: $P=\eta\chi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r(V/H)^2(H+H_p)$ (step 3b); for discrete DEAs, $\Delta F\propto\eta$.
  • Trend: full membrane 3.4, 4.1, 5.2, 8.0 mN at $\eta$ = 0.15, 0.24, 0.40, 0.70. Discrete 0.5, 0.9, 1.8, 4.0 mN. The sheet is estimated at 0.5, higher than a roll because it has no rolled air gaps.
  • Validate: flat strip of the real stack on the texture analyser, isometric, held at 1.6 kV. This is the cheapest and most useful next measurement.
