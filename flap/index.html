Which active wing changes lift more?

Two ways of putting dielectric elastomer actuators in a bat-inspired wing, compared over a flap cycle on the same wing size, stroke and voltage.

These are model predictions. The full membrane has not been built. Its advantage depends on an actuator efficiency $\eta$ that has not been measured on a flat sheet.

Plagiopatagiales: discrete DEAs

Rolled actuators in a passive membrane. Voltage relaxes the actuators, but the membrane in parallel carries most of the tension.

Full membrane

The skin is the actuator, with no passive membrane in parallel. It is tensioned before bonding, so voltage can remove most of its tension.

What this says

  • Weak results so far are expected. With a free trailing edge, the model keeps about 2 % of the actuator's force. Bowed DEAs in the videos fit this.
  • The full membrane leads in every case compared here, by about 5× at 4 m/s against an ideal discrete wing.
  • The lead narrows to 1.4× at 6 m/s, and to 1.7× at 4 m/s if the discrete wing's membrane is slack ($\lambda_{pre}$ = 1.00). So a discrete wing with trailing-edge tension and low pre-stretch is still worth one test.
  • Over the stroke: discrete DEAs follow the load, so they change lift most in the downstroke. The full membrane changes it about equally throughout.

What could change it

  • Flat-sheet $\eta$. At $\eta$ = 0.15, the full membrane falls to 2.8 mN at 4 m/s. Discrete DEAs at their bench cycle value give 0.5 mN.
  • Fabrication. The pre-stretch and whole-wing active film are untested, and one defect could disable a panel.
  • Flutter and stall. Neither is modelled. Both matter most on the slack, high-camber states.
How far to trust the comparison: the two wings use different models, a 3D finite-element and vortex-lattice model against a verified section model scaled to the same flat-wing lift. The figure shows one active layer on the earlier shape. The free-edge row multiplies the ideal result by an estimated 0.02. Differences of tens of percent mean little; differences of 5× do.
