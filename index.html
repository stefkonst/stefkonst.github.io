Stefania Konstantinidi, Ph.D.

Postdoctoral Research Fellow, Department of Mechanical Engineering & Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, Massachusetts Institute of Technology

I build soft actuators where motion is physically encoded into the material itself. By designing fiber-reinforced elastomers, I program how the material bends, twists, or contracts, and I apply that to mechanically active implants designed to restore facial movement after paralysis, and to bioinspired, morphing robotic wings.

Background

I did my Ph.D. in Robotics, Control and Intelligent Systems at EPFL, where I built the facial-reanimation actuators with Yves Perriard and Thomas Martinez, after an M.Sc. and B.Sc. there in Robotics and Microengineering. I'm now a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at MIT, jointly appointed in Mechanical Engineering and EECS, working with Raphaël Zufferey's Aerial and Underwater Robotics Lab and Yufeng Chen's Soft and Micro Robotics Lab on an SNSF Postdoc.Mobility Fellowship. Before the Ph.D. I co-led a medtech start-up project on concussion diagnosis during my master's, and helped build a diagnostic device that won the iGEM Grand Prize.

Outside the lab I'm usually in the mountains: trail running, ski touring (training for the Patrouille des Glaciers), or out fishing.

Research

I design actuators by borrowing directly from how muscle works in biological tissue, and I apply what I learn back to the implants and robots I build. The same idea shows up in a face and in a wing: put the actuation in the compliant surface itself, not in a rigid joint somewhere else. Longer term, I want to take that further, embedding fine, distributed musculature into conventional, rigid-skeleton robots.

Patent filed Journal cover, AMT 2025 Clinical partnership · USZ

Mechanically active implants for facial reanimation

Reinforced dielectric elastomer implants designed to restore expressions, like smiling and blinking, through directional deformation analogous to facial musculature itself. They displace tissue directly, with no motors and no gearboxes. The work spans actuator design, dynamic modeling, and real-time control, through to acute in vivo actuation and first steps towards triggering from a peripheral nerve interface, developed with the University Hospital Zurich Department of Plastic Surgery (Prof. Nicole Lindenblatt).

DEyeA: Artificial muscles for the restoration of eye blinking, Advanced Materials Technologies, 2025 (cover article) · Electromechanical device for actuating a patient's eyelid, International Patent Application WO2026028097
Best Paper, ICEMS 2023 TU Dresden collaboration

How reinforcement architecture governs deformation

A systematic investigation of buckling instabilities and necking in fiber-reinforced dielectric elastomer actuators: understanding, predicting, and ultimately designing around the structural instabilities that emerge when a soft membrane is both stretched and electrically actuated. The videos show a reinforced membrane buckling as voltage is applied, the structural signature these studies decode.

Investigation of buckling instabilities in fiber-reinforced DEAs, Composites Science and Technology, 2024 · Elimination of necking and aspect-ratio dependence by continuous fiber reinforcement, Advanced Robotics Research, 2026
New direction · MIT SNSF Postdoc.Mobility Fellowship Best Poster, HAMER 2026

An active, bat-inspired compliant wing membrane

Bat wings are among nature's most refined aeroelastic structures: unlike insect and bird wings, or the articulated rigid skeletons and passive membranes of current micro-air vehicles, bat flight is powered by soft, active membranes with embedded musculature that controls camber throughout the flap cycle. This new postdoctoral direction, independently funded by a Swiss National Science Foundation Postdoc.Mobility Fellowship, builds the engineering analog: active, fiber-reinforced membranes that encode wing shape directly into the material rather than through discrete control surfaces. The work is still unpublished, so details are necessarily brief for now.

Invited Rising Star Talk, "Embedding a Musculature in Compliant Systems," Northeastern Colloquium of Robotics, Princeton, 2026 · Best Poster Award, HAMER 2026

A two-person start-up project I co-led with a friend at the EPFL Innovation Park, carried out as our master's theses with business coaching from Marco Ruedi: a concussion diagnostic using near-infrared spectroscopy.
